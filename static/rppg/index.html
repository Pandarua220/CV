Measure your heart rate with your camera

Every heartbeat slightly changes the amount of blood in your facial skin, which changes the colour of the skin by a tiny fraction that your eyes can't see. Remote photoplethysmography (rPPG) recovers this pulse signal from an ordinary video. This demo runs the POS and DIS algorithms from my undergraduate lab's research on contactless vital-sign monitoring for neonates in the NICU, live in your browser.

Your video never leaves this device. All processing happens locally in the browser, and nothing is uploaded or stored.

How it works

Face trackingA light face detector (MediaPipe BlazeFace) locates the face in every frame.
28 skin patchesThe face region is divided into a 4 × 7 grid; mean R, G, B of each patch form the raw traces. The face position gives two motion traces.
ResamplingTraces are resampled to a uniform 30 Hz, since webcam frame timing varies.
Pre-processingIn sliding windows of 64 samples: divide by the mean (DC normalisation), 0.7–3 Hz zero-phase Butterworth band-pass, 3-tap smoothing.
Pulse extractionPOS or DIS turns each patch's colour traces into one pulse signal.
Patch selectionThe 5 patches with the highest SNR at the median dominant frequency are averaged (highlighted on the video).
Overlap-addNormalised window outputs are overlap-added (hop of 8 samples) into a continuous pulse waveform.
Heart rateA Hann-windowed spectrum of the last 10 s with 1 BPM resolution; its peak is the heart rate.

The two algorithms

POS: Plane-Orthogonal-to-Skin

Changes in light intensity scale all three colour channels together, along the direction of skin tone. POS projects the normalised RGB traces C onto a plane orthogonal to that direction, which removes intensity changes and keeps the pulse:

S = [ 0 1 −1 ; −2 1 1 ] · C
h = S1 + (σ(S1) / σ(S2)) · S2

The two projections carry the pulse with different distortions, and the “alpha tuning” step weighs them by their standard deviations so the distortions cancel out. POS needs no training and no assumption about the pulse colour.

DIS: Discriminative signatures

Blood volume changes produce a known, characteristic colour variation, the pulse blood volume (PBV) signature pbv. DIS appends extra “discriminative” channels that describe the distortions, here the head motion traces M, to the colour traces, and solves for the projection that matches the pulse signature while staying orthogonal to the distortions:

X = [ C ; M ]
h = [ pbv, 0, 0 ] · (X XT)+ · X

The zeros mean “no response to motion,” so motion-correlated colour changes are suppressed. Without motion channels this reduces to the PBV method. This demo uses pbv = [0.33, 0.77, 0.53].

Why it matters

Wired sensors such as ECG electrodes and pulse oximeter probes can irritate and damage the fragile skin of premature infants and are a source of infection. Our lab used camera-based rPPG to monitor heart rate, heart rate variability and blood oxygenation of neonates in the NICU without any contact, and builds on these signals for automated infant sleep staging.

This demo is for illustration only and is not a medical device. Lighting, skin tone, camera quality and movement all affect accuracy.
